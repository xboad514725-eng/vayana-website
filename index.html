A little context before you go

Explore the places.

Discover what makes each monument special and the tombs worth asking your guide about. Access to individual tombs can change and may require separate tickets.

Valley of the Kings

The New Kingdom royal necropolis, cut into the desert cliffs on Luxor’s West Bank. More than sixty tombs are known, with richly decorated corridors and burial chambers.

Highlights & tombs to know
  • KV2 — Ramesses IV: long corridors and astronomical decoration.
  • KV9 — Ramesses V & VI: celebrated ceiling scenes; separate ticket may apply.
  • KV17 — Seti I: exceptionally extensive decoration; separate ticket.
  • KV62 — Tutankhamun: famous discovery by Howard Carter in 1922; separate ticket.
  • KV11 — Ramesses III and KV6 — Ramesses IX are also notable where open.

Visit summary: Choose from the tombs open on the day. Your guide can explain royal burial beliefs, the Book of the Dead and the symbolism of the painted walls.

Valley of the Queens

Known in antiquity as Ta-Set-Neferu, this necropolis held queens, royal children and other members of the royal family.

Highlights & tombs to know
  • QV66 — Nefertari: renowned for exceptionally vivid wall paintings; special access and ticketing apply.
  • QV55 — Prince Amenherkhepshef: scenes of the prince with his father Ramesses III.
  • QV44 — Prince Khaemwaset: another important royal prince’s tomb.
  • QV52 — Queen Tyti is another notable tomb reference.

Visit summary: A focused visit to royal family burial art. Which tombs are open is subject to conservation and site rules.

Deir el-Medina

The workers’ village of the artisans who built and decorated royal tombs. Its remains and nearby private tombs offer a rare look at everyday life, family and craft in ancient Thebes.

Famous tombs & visit summary
  • TT1 — Sennedjem: celebrated painted burial chamber.
  • TT3 — Pashedu: known for its colourful decoration.
  • TT359 — Inherkhau (Inherkhâouy): an important Ramesside tomb with decorated chambers.
  • TT2 — Khabekhnet and TT4 — Qen are other notable names.

Visit summary: Explore the village remains, the small temple area and selected artisan tombs if open. The site is especially rewarding for visitors interested in the people behind royal monuments.

The Ramesseum

The mortuary temple of Ramesses II, famous for its monumental scale, fallen colossal statue and reliefs commemorating the Battle of Kadesh.

What you will see
  • First and second courtyards and surviving Osiride columns.
  • Hypostyle hall and decorated walls.
  • Reliefs of royal ceremonies and the Battle of Kadesh.
  • Remains of the colossal statue of Ramesses II.

Visit summary: A rewarding stop for architecture, royal propaganda and the legacy of Ramesses the Great. Allow time to appreciate the details rather than only photographing the facade.

Medinet Habu

The mortuary temple of Ramesses III, one of the best-preserved temple complexes on the West Bank, with massive gateways and extensive battle reliefs.

What you will see
  • The fortified entrance and high gate.
  • Large courtyards, halls and richly carved walls.
  • Reliefs depicting campaigns against the Sea Peoples and Libyans.
  • Religious scenes connected with Amun and royal funerary worship.

Visit summary: An excellent place to understand the military and religious story of Ramesses III. Look closely at the carved details across the pylons and inner courts.

Temple of Hatshepsut

Djeser-Djeseru, the “Holy of Holies”, rises in terraces against the cliffs of Deir el-Bahari. It was built for the female pharaoh Hatshepsut and designed by Senenmut.

Itinerary & highlights
  • Lower, middle and upper terraces linked by ramps.
  • Colonnades and reliefs showing the divine birth and the expedition to Punt.
  • Sanctuaries associated with Amun, Hathor and Anubis.

Visit summary: A striking combination of architecture and landscape. Your guide can explain Hatshepsut’s reign and the stories carved into the temple walls.

Karnak Temple

A vast complex of temples and chapels dedicated principally to Amun. Its Great Hypostyle Hall is a forest of 134 monumental columns.

Itinerary & highlights
  • Great Hypostyle Hall and its towering columns.
  • Obelisks of Hatshepsut and the sacred lake.
  • Precinct of Amun and the processional axis toward Luxor Temple.
  • Open-air museum, subject to ticket and opening arrangements.

Visit summary: Allow generous time: Karnak is a complex rather than a single building, and its history spans many dynasties.

Luxor Temple

A monumental temple largely developed under Amenhotep III and Ramesses II, linked to Karnak by the Avenue of Sphinxes and central to the Opet Festival.

Itinerary & highlights
  • Ramesses II’s pylon and statues.
  • Great Colonnade and Amenhotep III’s court.
  • Reliefs connected with the Opet Festival and divine kingship.
  • Later Roman-period traces within the complex.

Visit summary: Especially atmospheric in the late afternoon or evening, subject to opening hours and your schedule.

Luxor Museum

A carefully curated museum displaying sculpture, royal objects and finds from the Theban area. It offers a quieter, focused complement to the open-air temples.

Visit summary
  • Statues and objects from temples and tombs around Luxor.
  • Royal and religious sculpture with clear presentation.
  • A useful stop to connect the monuments with the objects found within them.

Planning note: Museum hours and ticketing may differ from temple sites; we confirm before the tour.

Valley of the Nobles

A broad name for private tomb cemeteries of officials, priests and administrators around the Theban necropolis. These tombs preserve vivid scenes of daily life, work, banquets and religious ritual.

Famous tombs to ask about
  • TT52 — Nakht: celebrated scenes of agriculture, music and offerings.
  • TT100 — Rekhmire: important scenes of administration, crafts and tribute.
  • TT55 — Ramose: elegant reliefs and an unfinished tomb chapel.
  • TT69 — Menna: well-known agricultural and family scenes.

Visit summary: The exact tombs available vary. Your guide can recommend those open and best suited to your interests.
